Abstract Review - Boundary-Aware Rationale Selection

Editable abstract draft with reviewer comments

Abstract

Abstract Draft

Rationale-based distillation uses explanations generated by large language models as auxiliary supervision for training compact student models. While prior work shows that generated rationales can improve textual entailment and reasoning tasks, their quality is not guaranteed at the example level: a rationale may be fluent but weakly grounded, label-inconsistent, or unsuitable for learning. This paper extends the original rationale-distillation framework with a boundary-aware rationale selection pipeline. The proposed method groups multiple rationale candidates for the same input, aggregates their predicted labels through weighted voting, estimates example difficulty using agreement and vote margin, and selects training rationales using source-label compatibility, input grounding, answer support, brevity, and confidence signals. The main variant, Boundary-Mix, keeps one high-quality rationale from easy and boundary examples while removing unstable hard examples. Experiments on ESNLI and CommonsenseQA show that Boundary-Mix improves the original rationale-distilled baseline from 83.93% to 84.16% on ESNLI and from 60.36% to 62.41% on CommonsenseQA under the same compact student-model setting. A same-family comparison further shows that different selection rules affect datasets differently: Boundary-Bridge achieves stronger ESNLI performance, while Boundary-Mix gives the most consistent completed result across both datasets. These findings suggest that rationale-based distillation benefits not only from generating explanations, but also from selecting explanations that are reliable and suitable for student learning.
